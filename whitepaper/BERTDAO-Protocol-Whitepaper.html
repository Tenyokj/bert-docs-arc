Abstract

BERTDAO Protocol is programmable, stablecoin-native infrastructure for coordinating proposals and capital. BERT V2 operates a global grant flow: stake-backed idea intake, round-based USDC voting, explicit reserve accounting, and reviewer-gated milestone payout. BERT V3 introduces an isolated Community Layer: each Community has its own immutable governance Hub and USDC Treasury, local membership, validator review, binary and slate voting, and quorum-protected administrative actions. Defined V3 protocol inflows route into the existing V2 global reserve without merging Community execution balances or governance state.

The protocol is deliberately modular. Proposal metadata, vote lifecycle, token accounting, grant payout state, role authority, reputation, and progression each have a defined onchain owner. This reduces ambiguity for integrators and auditors: the interface is not the source of truth, and an offchain index does not replace a contract read when finality matters.

01 / Problem and thesis

Capital decisions should be inspectable, not trapped in spreadsheets and chat.

Grant programs and community treasuries often split a single decision across forms, informal moderation, spreadsheets, multisig messages, and offchain payout tracking. That fragmentation makes it difficult to answer basic questions: who proposed an allocation, what capital supported it, what was decided, where are the funds, and what evidence unlocked a payment?

The coordination problem

A useful treasury process needs more than a vote. It needs a way to resist low-cost spam, a lifecycle that cannot be rewritten casually, capital accounting that distinguishes deposits from obligations, and an execution process that does not release all funds before work is evidenced.

01 / INTAKE

Unpriced proposal spam

Without an economic commitment or review path, a public intake surface can become a queue of low-signal submissions.

02 / DECISION

Opaque capital signaling

A social poll does not explain how much capital was committed, which rules applied, or what happens on a failed result.

03 / EXECUTION

One-shot payout risk

A winning proposal is not evidence that all future work has been delivered. Treasury release needs its own accountable state machine.

BERT thesis

BERT treats proposal intake, selection, and payment as connected but distinct protocol stages. The V2 Global Funding Layer provides the shared grant and reserve machinery. The V3 Community Layer lets independent groups operate their own local governance and execution treasury while remaining connected to a defined global reserve route.

A proposal is not merely content. It is a lifecycle object with an author, a capital commitment, an outcome, and—when funded—an accountable release path.

What BERT is and is not

BERT isBERT is not

02 / Principles and scope

Explicit state, explicit capital, explicit authority.

Design principles

Single source of truth by domain

Idea metadata belongs to IdeaRegistryUpgradeable; round state belongs to VotingSystemUpgradeable; treasury and reserve accounting belongs to FundingPoolUpgradeable; payout state belongs to GrantManagerUpgradeable; role assignments belong to RolesRegistryUpgradeable.

Economic actions are visible

Proposal deposits, vote commitments, execution balances, reserve routes, validator rewards, and refund liabilities are distinct accounting concepts. A displayed token balance must not be treated as generic free liquidity.

Local autonomy without silent pooling

A V3 Community has isolated state and custody. Its paired Treasury can only make defined protocol-reserve transfers through the authenticated V2 reserve route; a Community's membership stake and execution balance are not global reserve capital.

Human trust remains a boundary

Contracts can enforce configured thresholds and lifecycle rules, but reviewer quality, admin behavior, configuration choices, and future identity-provider operation remain social and operational trust assumptions.

Scope of this paper

This paper documents the public BERT V2 and V3 architecture as implemented in the core repository and Arc Testnet deployment manifest. It intentionally does not define token economics, a tradable token, speculative yield, NFT rewards, airdrops, legal compliance policy, or a final mainnet timetable.

03 / System architecture

Two connected layers, clear execution boundaries.

BERT V2 and V3 are complementary. V2 is the global funding and grant layer. V3 is the factory-created Community Layer. The connection is intentionally narrow: authenticated V3 Community Treasuries can route defined protocol inflows to the V2 reserve.

Only defined protocol-reserve inflows from an active Treasury route to FundingPoolUpgradeable.receiveCommunityReserve. V2 does not custody arbitrary local Community balances.

Contract deployment model

V2 core modules are upgradeable and interdependent. Their safety depends on storage compatibility, dependency wiring, role assignment, and controlled administration. V3 takes a different isolation approach: the shared CommunityFactory is upgradeable, while every deployed CommunityHub and CommunityTreasury is immutable for that Community. The Factory has no post-activation governance authority over a Community.

Source of truth and indexers

Contract storage and successful chain execution are authoritative. The Graph provides convenient event-derived discovery and UI views, but users and integrators should fall back to contract reads for security-sensitive state, especially role status, paused state, claimability, balances, and settlement status. The public Arc RPC may prune historical logs; subgraphs reduce that read-path fragility for historical discovery but do not alter execution truth.

USDC denomination

Economic amounts are denominated in the configured USDC-compatible ERC-20's native units. Arc USDC is displayed with six decimals. Interfaces must not assume 18 decimals for user input, formatting, or allowance calculations.

04 / BERT V2

Global funding layer: proposals, rounds, reserve, and staged grants.

BERT V2 coordinates a protocol-wide grant lifecycle. Its modules keep intake, decision, accounting, payout, permissions, and social progression separate so no one contract becomes an ambiguous source for every kind of state.

1. Staked idea creation

An author calls createIdea on the Idea Registry with non-empty title and description, optional link, and an amount at least equal to authorMinStake. The Registry checks the configured FundingPool, USDC balance and allowance, initializes reputation when necessary, then causes the author stake to be pulled into FundingPool. The new idea begins in Pending.

2. Round formation and voting

An operator starts a round only when enough unused ideas exist. The default implementation batches 30 ideas and opens a one-day voting window, but these are configurable policy values rather than economic guarantees. A voter can make one vote in a round, cannot vote for their own idea, must meet the current minimum stake, and is subject to the configured verified-human policy and per-vote cap when enabled.

3. Deterministic resolution

After the window ends, any eligible caller may settle the round. The highest recorded vote total wins. If no idea receives votes, the round resolves with no winner and the included ideas follow the rejected path. The Voting System updates lifecycle state, triggers outcome-linked reputation effects, and registers winning voters for progression.

Interpretation rule. A valid integration must present reserve, active round capital, locked author stake, and already-released payout as separate concepts. Collapsing them into one "treasury available" number would misrepresent the protocol's obligations.

Grant claim and milestone releases

A winning author may call claimGrant(roundId) only after a qualifying round is ended and the grant has not already been claimed. The contract determines the winning idea and reads its round-specific capital. The current default author share is 95%; the remaining 5% follows the protocol share path. The author share is not released at once.

For the in-process stage, the implementation requires 3 approvals from up to 5 reviewers. For the completion stage, it requires 2 approvals from up to 3 reviewers. The author cannot review their own work. A rejected milestone request records a rejection time and uses a 48-hour resubmission cooldown. These rules exist to make later payout conditional on evidence and review, not simply on winning an earlier vote.

Reputation and progression

V2 separates reputation from progression. Reputation records outcome-linked performance and is not a treasury balance or vote multiplier. Voter progression counts winning votes and can grant user roles through the role system. Current thresholds in the implementation are 20 winning votes for CURATOR_ROLE and 60 for REVIEWER_ROLE. This reduces fully arbitrary assignment, but it does not eliminate social coordination or admin-risk concerns.

V2 policy surfaces

Stake minima, round size, duration, author share, vote caps, verified-human gating, trusted signer, and module dependencies are operational policy. Any future governance process must treat parameter changes as security- and UX-relevant changes, rehearse them on a staging environment, and record their effect in a changelog.

05 / BERT V3

Community Layer: isolated governance with local USDC custody.

BERT V3 enables an organization or group to create its own stake-gated governance domain. Every active Community is a paired immutable CommunityHub and CommunityTreasury, registered and activated by the shared CommunityFactory.

Why V3 is separate from V2

V2 organizes global funding rounds and grants. V3 is designed for local Community governance: a Community needs its own membership boundary, its own Admin and Validator set, its own proposal review, its own execution balance, and its own withdrawal quorum. Mixing those local rules into the V2 global state would weaken isolation and make policy harder to reason about.

Role exclusivity and membership

An address may not simultaneously hold more than one active Community role state: Admin, Validator, or Member. A wallet joins by approving the paired Treasury and calling joinCommunity; the Treasury checks the shared human verifier and locks the immutable entry stake. Membership exit is explicit: request first, wait through the Community's cooldown, then finalize only after role conflicts, active proposals, and uncleared vote locks are gone.

Community-local roles are intentionally separate from V2 RolesRegistryUpgradeable, reputation, and progression. A V2 reviewer or curator has no automatic V3 authority. This avoids importing a global status into a local governance trust model without a Community decision.

Validator eligibility

A former Member can become eligible for nomination after accumulating the Community's immutable number of winning Member proposal points. That threshold is selected at creation and must be between 1 and 100. Eligibility is only a precondition: an Admin quorum still adds the Validator, and role exclusivity must be satisfied. The intent is to connect validator eligibility to demonstrated local outcomes without treating an automated metric as an automatic role grant.

Pause-aware time

V3 uses a Community clock that excludes paused time. Validation windows, voting windows, validator epochs, and protected-action expiry are measured against that clock. When archived, the clock freezes. This prevents a pause introduced during an incident from silently consuming deadlines that Members, Validators, or Admins could not act on.

05 / BERT V3 continued

From proposal review to settlement.

Member proposals have a validation gate

Members create either binary or slate proposals. A Member proposal immediately locks the configured USDC anti-spam bond and enters PendingValidation. Validators cast one approval or rejection during the configured validation window. The proposal passes when approvals reach the immutable threshold; it fails early when the remaining possible approvals cannot reach that threshold; otherwise it can be finalized after the deadline using the approvals received.

On validator rejection, the Member proposal bond is routed to the global BERT reserve. If the proposal passes validation, its bond is returned when the later vote or slate round settles, whether it wins or fairly loses. The distinction is intentional: validation rejection is a moderation decision; a fairly considered democratic loss should not use the same economic penalty.

Each Member chooses one side and commits an amount at least equal to the Community minimum and no greater than the protocol-wide cap of 10,000 USDC. YES must strictly exceed NO to win; a tie follows the NO/refund path. A proposal author cannot vote on their own proposal. The Hub records the governance state; only the paired Treasury moves the corresponding USDC.

YES outcome

For a winning Member binary proposal, YES stake is settled into Community execution funds and the configured validator-reward share; NO stake routes to the global BERT reserve. The public V3 design documents a 15% default validator share and 85% execution share, but the actual immutable validatorRewardShareBps selected at Community creation is authoritative. Admin-origin binary proposals did not require validator review, so their local winning stake goes entirely to execution.

NO or tie outcome

YES stake and the configured NO-side rejection fee route to the global reserve. The remaining NO stake becomes a pull-based refund liability; each eligible NO voter must claim their own refund. The rejection fee is immutable per Community and cannot exceed 10% (1,000 basis points).

Slate rounds

Admins can open a homogeneous slate round containing approved Admin slate proposals or validator-approved Member slate proposals. A Member selects exactly one proposal and stakes once. The highest total wins; ties resolve deterministically in favor of the earliest proposal in the supplied round order. Slate rounds do not have a NO side. Their committed stake settles locally into execution and, for Member-origin rounds, the configured validator reward route.

Community Treasury: custody follows validated state.

The Hub never custodies USDC. It validates lifecycle transitions and instructs its paired Treasury. The Treasury separates funds by obligation so membership stake, bonds, escrow, refunds, validator rewards, and executable balances cannot be casually mixed.

Validator reward epochs

Validator rewards are measured in epochs. Member-proposal validation cases are counted, and an active-validator threshold determines whether a Validator is eligible. After the epoch and associated validation window close, rewards are finalized as equal claimable shares for active Validators. Rounding dust—or all rewards when no Validator is active—returns to execution balance. Claims are pull-based and protected against double claim.

Execution withdrawals

A current Admin can create a withdrawal request from available execution balance. The request records recipient, amount, reason, metadata URI, and the creator's first approval; it also reserves the amount immediately. Other current Admins approve once each. The Treasury transfers only after the Hub's immutable Admin threshold is met. A pending withdrawal can be cancelled only through the separate quorum-protected Admin action process, preventing one Admin from unilaterally undoing a reserved action.

Archive safety

Archive is a protected action. Community lifecycle changes, roster changes, pause/resume, and withdrawal cancellation use the linked CommunityAdminActions library through the Hub. The requester contributes the first approval; the immutable threshold is evaluated at execution time; requests expire after seven active Community days; paused time does not count. An Admin handover intentionally takes two actions: add the replacement, then remove the former Admin.

06 / Human verification

Provider-agnostic onchain gate, explicit offchain trust boundary.

BERT does not embed a vendor SDK in its Solidity core. Instead, PoPVerifierUpgradeable records signed verification attestations and exposes a narrow isVerifiedHuman(account) read. Protected contracts decide whether to require that read for a configured action.

Attestation model

Verification path
Wallet requests verification
→
Backend verifies provider-specific proof
→
Trusted signer creates wallet-bound, expiring attestation
→
Wallet submits payload to PoPVerifier

The verifier validates the trusted signer signature, the wallet binding, nonce and expiry, then stores the active record. The contract is intentionally provider-agnostic: policy can evolve without coupling every protocol module to external identity-provider code. The tradeoff is explicit: the backend, signer key management, proof-validation implementation, and provider policy become a meaningful trust boundary.

Protected actions

In V2, the live policy can require verified human status for voting and idea creation. In V3, the Factory requires a verified creator to reserve a Community and the Community Treasury requires a verified wallet before locking entry stake and activating Membership. Read-only calls and asset-recovery flows are not indiscriminately gated, so a verification policy does not strand an existing user's funds.

07 / Security and operations

Correct contracts still require correct operations.

BERT's primary risks are not limited to conventional Solidity bugs. The protocol is multi-contract and upgradeable: security also depends on authority, configuration, dependency wiring, identity-signing operations, indexer behavior, and the social quality of reviewers and validators.

Security controls in the current design

  • Explicit role gates for treasury, lifecycle, reputation, and cross-contract actions.
  • Forward-only V2 idea lifecycle and stage-aware payout records to resist duplicate or skipped grant release.
  • Separated accounting buckets for V2 reserve and V3 local obligations.
  • SafeERC20 transfers and reentrancy protection around sensitive token flows.
  • Pause controls on critical V2 modules, and pause-aware V3 Community time.
  • Immutable V3 per-Community configuration, paired Hub/Treasury activation checks, and Factory authentication for reserve inflows.
  • Admin quorum for V3 roster changes, pause/resume, archive, and withdrawal cancellation.
  • Tests covering core V2 flows, V3 Factory/Hub/Treasury flows, edge cases, upgrade behavior, and non-standard token scenarios.

Upgrade discipline

Storage compatibility is necessary but insufficient. New storage must be appended; inherited layout and storage gaps must be preserved; proxy target and admin must be verified. After any upgrade, operators must validate live implementation address, dependency addresses, role assignments, human-verification configuration, idea creation, voting, grant claimability, and the relevant V3 reserve boundary. A rollback is a controlled re-upgrade, not an automatic undo mechanism.

Disclosure and incident response

Public source repositories use private GitHub vulnerability reporting for security disclosures. A production runbook must preserve the ability to pause the minimum necessary V2 modules, capture state and transaction evidence, rotate affected credentials, patch and rehearse remediation, and publish an incident summary after safe resolution.

Audit status. The current testnet codebase has automated and manual test coverage, static-analysis work, public source review, and a disclosure path. It has not yet completed the independent smart-contract audit required before any mainnet capital deployment. No reader should infer otherwise.

08 / Data and developer interfaces

Contracts execute. Indexers make history usable.

Integration rule

An integration should use direct contract reads for final, security-sensitive data and use indexed events for discovery, activity history, search, and performant lists. The intended client model is not to scan all logs from genesis through a public RPC on every page load; such requests can fail on pruned RPC history.

V3 discovery model

The V3 Subgraph indexes the Factory and uses Community Hub event templates so newly activated Communities can become discoverable without hardcoding each Hub address into the frontend. This supports Community directories, role rosters reconstructed from role events, proposal history, rounds, Treasury activity, and lifecycle displays. Contract reads remain necessary where a current record decides whether a write action may proceed.

Developer safeguards

  • Format Arc USDC with six decimals; do not assume an 18-decimal token.
  • Distinguish a proxy address from an implementation address; users interact with the proxy/current contract address.
  • Preflight allowance, current verification, pause state, role, timing, and status before asking a wallet to sign.
  • After a transaction, wait for receipt and refresh authoritative contract state. A subgraph can lag behind chain finality.
  • Do not make client-side constants the final policy authority; read configuration from the relevant contract.

10 / Mainnet conditions

Mainnet is a gate, not a date.

The correct launch criterion is not whether BERT can be deployed. The code can be deployed today; a responsible production release requires evidence that its security, operations, identity policy, economics, and user flows are ready for assets with real value.

Testnet work that matters

Before a mainnet release, BERT should recruit real testnet users and small design partners rather than optimize for passive social metrics. The useful evidence is completed end-to-end flows, reproducible bugs, failed assumptions, data about user confusion, validator/admin workload, and documented operating procedures. This is also the right environment to rework the UI with an experienced frontend engineer.

Community and contributor model

Core contracts, backend, frontend, and documentation are public under GPL-3.0. Public visibility does not grant direct push rights. Contributors work through forks and pull requests; maintainers retain merge and deployment control. Security reports should remain private until a patch and coordinated disclosure are ready.

No implied launch promise. This whitepaper intentionally does not name a mainnet date, token, yield, reward, or valuation. Any such statement should be made only after the relevant technical, legal, economic, and operational decisions are finalized.

Protocol invariants and glossary.

Selected invariants

AreaInvariant
V2 state ownershipIdeas, rounds, treasury/reserve accounting, payouts, roles, reputation, and progression each have a designated contract owner.
V2 lifecycleAn idea enters through Pending; only valid round resolution or grant transitions move it forward; rejected and completed states are terminal.
V2 payoutsA grant tranche must not be released twice, and later stages cannot bypass earlier stage ordering.
V3 role stateAn address cannot hold more than one active Community state among Admin, Validator, and Member.
V3 settlementA binary proposal or slate round settles once; validator reward claims and NO refunds settle once per eligible account.
V3 custodyMembership lock, bond lock, escrow, refund liability, validator rewards, and execution funds are separately accounted and not interchangeable.
V3 authorityNo external wallet can invoke linked Admin action implementation directly; Hub rechecks caller and lifecycle rules at execution.
V3 reserve routingV2 FundingPool accepts Community reserve inflows only from a Treasury registered as active by Factory.
